## Biomarkers to viscosity

Biomarker analyses are well established as quantitative proxies for biodegradation and for predicting oil physical properties such as viscosity. Multiple studies report strong empirical and statistical relationships between specific biomarker ratios or recovered biomarker parameters and in situ viscosity or producibility indicators.

- **High-correlation empirical models** show that biomarker biodegradation parameters (e.g., Pr/Ph, nC17+nC18 ratios and hopane-based ratios) correlate tightly with measured viscosities, with reported correlation coefficients up to 0.96 in targeted heavy-oil suites [1].  
- **Reservoir mapping and management** approaches converted sidewall-core biomarker measurements into lateral/vertical viscosity and gravity maps used for well placement and completion design [2].  
- **Biodegradation staging and standardized scales** (PM scale and related parameter approaches) are commonly used to rank biodegradation severity and to select biomarker parameters suitable for proxying viscosity in different degradation regimes [3].  
- **Parameter recovery and deconvolution** methods have been developed to "recover" original biomarker signatures in extremely degraded oils, improving correlations with source and fluid properties and enabling quantitative reservoir characterization despite severe alteration [4].  
- **Multivariate statistical methods** (e.g., PLS and related regression) have been applied to relate compositional/biomarker data sets to viscosity while accounting for multicollinearity and sample handling effects [5] [6] [7].

These studies establish a state of the art in translating molecular biomarker depletion into engineering-relevant proxies (viscosity, gravity, producibility), but they do not extend to thermodynamic metrics in the supplied literature [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7].
