## The Gouy-Stodola Thermodynamic Derivation in MANCO-EX

The Gouy-Stodola thermodynamic derivation as presented in the manuscript is detailed below:

* **Control Volume Definition**: The framework defines an open control volume $\Omega_{\text{res}}$ encompassing the liquid crude oil phase within the reservoir pore space at the oil-water contact (OWC) [1].
* **Dead State Specification**: The reference environment (dead state) is explicitly specified at standard conditions of $T_0 = 298.15\text{ K}$ ($25.0^\circ\text{C}$) and $P_0 = 1.013\text{ bar}$ ($1\text{ atm}$) [2].
* **Pressure Exergy Component**: The pressure exergy component is discussed and shown to be minor. Specifically, while deep reservoir fluids exist under elevated static pressure ($P_{\text{res}} = 80\text{--}250\text{ bar}$), the pressure exergy component $e_{x,P} = v_m(P_{\text{res}} - P_0) \approx 0.02\text{ kJ/mol}$ is smaller by more than three orders of magnitude than the thermochemical exergy destroyed during hydrocarbon oxidation ($\Delta X_d = 5.40\text{--}10.68\text{ kJ/mol}$) [3].
* **Algebraic Pathway from Biomarkers to $\Delta X_d$**: The step-by-step algebraic pathway converts biomarker concentrations to the Specific Exergy Loss Index ($\Delta X_d$, in $\text{kJ/mol}$) using the following equations:
  1. First, the multi-tiered cascade function $\Phi_{\text{cascade}}$ is computed by combining the biomarker tiers [4]:
     $$\Phi_{\text{cascade}} = \alpha \cdot \left[\frac{\sum[\text{OrgAcids}]}{C_0}\right] + \beta \cdot \left[\frac{\text{1-MP} + \text{2-MP}}{\text{3-MP} + \text{9-MP}}\right] + \gamma \cdot \left[\frac{\text{C}_{20}\text{TAS}}{\text{C}_{28}\text{TAS}}\right] + \delta \cdot \ln \left(1 + \frac{\text{Asphaltenes}}{\text{Saturates} + \epsilon}\right) \quad \text{(Eq. 12)}$$
  2. Then, the Specific Exergy Loss Index is calculated as [5] [6]:
     $$\Delta X_d = R \cdot T_0 \cdot \ln(1 + \Phi_{\text{cascade}}) \quad \text{(Eq. 11)}$$
* **Entropy Generation Mechanisms**: The entropy generation mechanisms are identified as both mixing and chemical/thermal reaction components. The mixing component $\Delta S_{\text{mix}} = R \sum x_i \ln(x_i / \gamma_i x_i^0)$ accounts for configurational entropy generation resulting from aliphatic depletion and polar resino-asphaltic enrichment [7]. The total exergy loss is related to the total entropy generation $\Delta S_{\text{gen}}$ via [8]:
  $$\Delta X_d(T_{\text{res}}) = T_0 \cdot \Delta S_{\text{gen}} = T_0 \cdot \left[\Delta S_{\text{mix}} + \frac{\Delta H_{\text{rxn}} - \Delta G_{\text{rxn}}}{T_{\text{res}}}\right] \ge 0 \quad \text{(Eq. 2)}$$
* **Theorem Citations**: For the Gouy-Stodola theorem, the manuscript cites Gouy (1889) , Stodola (1910) , Bejan (1982) , and Bejan (2016) [1] collectively [9].
* **Eyring Model Justification**: The Eyring transition-state rate theory is applied to connect exergy destruction to dynamic viscosity [10]. It is justified for high-viscosity heavy oils ($10^3\text{--}10^5\text{ cP}$) at reservoir temperatures ($30\text{--}80^\circ\text{C}$) because viscous dissipation in biodegraded oils is governed by the thermal activation energy required for molecules to jump past structural obstacles created by pi-pi stacking of asphaltenic nano-aggregates, referencing Macías-Salinas (2009)  and Andrade & Rajagopal (2018)  (along with Miadonye & Amadu (2024) ) [10] [11].
